_A look back (and forward) as I celebrate 25 years of service with Nationwide_

### 25 Years? But You're Not Old Enough (I Hear You Cry)

In some ways it seems impossible that I could have been working at Nationwide for 25 years, probably because I don't want to admit to possibly being old enough to have achieved such a milestone, but when I stop and reflect on all that's happened since I walked through the doors of Nationwide House in 2001, it makes complete sense - many of my earliest memories at the 'wide seem like a lifetime ago now.

I'd imagine it's quite unusual in this day and age to have spent so long at one employer, and I'm sure many of my friends must think me a little odd for it. So I thought this anniversary was a perfect opportunity to delve into why I'm still at Nationwide. Of course, I've often been tempted to spread my wings - and have been seriously tempted by opportunities at 'sexier' tech companies. So why am I still here?

I think it boils down to three main things - in decreasing order of importance: -
- The people
- The growth opportunities
- The 'package'


---
### The People

A theme throughout my time at Nationwide has been the many excellent people I've been able to work alongside.

I started in 2001 in the old (old old?) technology trainee scheme and was lucky enough to be in a large enough cohort that many of us worked, and in many ways, grew up together. Working in desktop support I got to wander the building all day, meeting people from all departments, creating many enduring friendships along the way.

When I moved into a Windows server support role my "buddy", Paul, was incredibly generous with his time and knowledge and told me so much about things I had zero experience of. We'd often work out of hours together and it was always a hoot.

Similarly, when I joined the cloud team and entered the world of infrastructure as code, I was so fortunate to work alongside (in particular) Jay and Matt. Again, their patience and guidance were invaluable. Their influence hugely impacted the opinionated views I hold to this day on how IAC should be done.

---
### The Growth Opportunities

In pretty much every role I've been in, innovation and thinking outside the box has been encouraged.

Some of the stand-out pieces of work I remember with particular pride are the ones where I've gone off on my own and just "had a go".

#### Unknown Application Issue Discovery

From exploring web server log data in Splunk and looking for obscure http sub-status codes, I was able to eventually prove particular edge case scenarios that would consistently fail for an application used by branch staff for a particular account type with particular conditions due to the length of the XML payload. These errors were so seldom that they were never spotted, but they could be reliably reproduced given the results of me poking around Splunk.

### An Unlikely Parallel

It was back in the autumn of 2001 when I first walked through the doors of Nationwide House as one of the newest members of the technology trainee scheme (as it was back then). Just a couple of weeks later the world would also be introduced to JD, Turk, and Elliot who walked through the doors of Sacred Heart as interns.

Like those interns in Scrubs, I bumbled and stumbled my away around the real world, fresh out of education and trying to fake it til I make it. JD famously had Dr Cox as his mentor. In my case, I was the thorn in the side of Andy, probably asking too many questions all day, every day.

It wasn't until this year that the parallel entered my consciousness, as Scrubs returned for a new series - this time with the former interns now in much more senior roles; the students had become the teachers - just as I was about to reach that landmark 25th anniversary.

And as I sit back and think about these parallels, it's almost uncanny.

### The Early Years (2001-2009)

As a young buck entering the workplace at 18, going into a huge head office which housed several thousand employees was initially quite (very) daunting. But it's amazing how quickly those fears can be released when you are surrounded by good people. Working initially in desktop support, I was given the chance to get out and about and fix people's PC and laptop issues all over the building - from people in the call centre (which was then located in the head office), to HR, to Risk, Mortgages...you name it.

When I first started at the 'wide, Windows NT4 was the OS of choice that I was tasked with supporting. That's incredibly scary because, while in many ways it seems like 25 years have flown by, thinking back to the days of that clunky old OS and things like CRT monitors seems like a lifetime ago.

I was lucky to work alongside a good mix of people - some 3-5 years older than me, some in their 30s; in a lot of ways, it didn't feel like I was really going to *work* work because really, it was a lot of fun. And actually, it wasn't just my immediate colleagues that made it fun. Back in the early 2000's it seemed like everyone took themselves, and work, a lot less seriously. It was almost like a (really big) family run business. That meant I struck up so many friendships with people from all over the building. It really was a great time.

### The Wilderness Years (2009-2016)
In 2009, the desktop support function was outsourced to ComputaCenter. At some point early on in the contract, a colleague and I were put at risk of redundancy. We went through the whole consultation process and were eventually given a leaving date. It was incredibly stressful but one of the things I remember most was how supportive the Union were throughout. Eventually, for whatever reason, they pulled the plug on the redundancy and I was retained.

The shape of the support function changed somewhat but a few of us more experienced guys eventually made our way to a 3rd line support role where we'd deal with things like Group Policy and the Windows Firewall. At the same time, a subset of that team was also responsible for supporting the directors and their various devices. This was hugely eye-opening for so many reasons. I'll leave it at that 😁
